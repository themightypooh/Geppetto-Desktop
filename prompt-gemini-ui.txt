GEMINI - LANE C - UI CONCEPT. Read a lot, write one doc.
=====================================================

WHAT THIS IS
  A design brief, not an implementation job. Joshua has decided the UI
  should be reconceived rather than ported, and he wants an independent
  head to concept it before anyone writes a widget. You are that head.
  Your deliverable is ONE document. No source edits, no code.

  He said, in these words: "i think i want it different, gimme a handoff
  doc for gemini to help me concept out a new better UI". So the
  existing UI is the thing being replaced on purpose. Treat it as
  evidence of what the app must be able to do. Do not treat it as a
  layout to reproduce.

FIRST, THE PROJECT
  "Geppetto" is two tools in one repo:

    Effigy     - a parametric modeller. Its document is a plain text
                 .effigy file, one line per feature, git-diffable.
                 Features are a tree: blob/surface-nets hulls, remesh
                 and decimate, UV projection and organic unwrap, lofts
                 and profiles, sweeps, fillets, mirrors, patterns,
                 kitbash parts, shape keys, paint/vertex colour.

    Marionette - an animation editor. Skeletons, skin weights, easing
                 curves, auto-key, two-bone and chain IK, joint limits,
                 springy secondary motion, clip baking, .riganim clips.

  Both are being turned into ONE native Linux desktop app with no game
  engine and no editor dependency. That app is what you are designing
  the UI for.

WHAT JOSHUA ACTUALLY WANTS OUT OF IT
  He is a solo animator and modeller. He wants one tool he reaches for,
  not a suite. He is putting together an animation reel and wants to be
  paid for rigging and animation work. He works on one desktop, 16:9,
  Linux/Wayland, mouse and keyboard, and he is not a keyboard-shortcut
  maximalist.

HARD CONSTRAINTS - a concept that breaks these is not a concept
  - ONE native window, GPU-first, no engine. No website, no Electron.
  - BUTTER SMOOTH, and that is a measured requirement, not a vibe: the
    whole viewport currently holds 60 fps with p99 under 19 ms and zero
    frames over 33 ms. The UI must fit in that budget. Say what your
    proposal costs per frame and where the cost lands. Never propose a
    design that only works if the viewport drops to 30 fps.
  - No s&box assets or chrome. Nothing named "citizen".
  - Immediate-mode provisioning is available but not mandatory. ImGui.NET
    is the assumed toolkit because your own imgui-port.md already mapped
    onto it. If you think a different approach is the right call, say so
    and argue it - with the reason, and with what it costs.

WHAT TO WRITE - ONE FILE
  /home/poo/games/projects/geppetto-desktop/specs/ui-concept.md

  Ask yourself the question the current UI never asked: what is the SPINE
  of this tool? Roughly, which five or six interactions carry most of
  the work? Design those first and let them dictate everything else,
  instead of starting from a list of panels.

  Include, in this order:

  1. WHAT IT IS FOR, in one page. Who uses it, for what, and what it is
     deliberately not for. If you think the two tools should be one
     document or two, say which and why - that is a UI decision as much
     as a data one. There is evidence for it in the document above: a
     character's .effigy file is already organised like a body, 617
     pieces of one, named by region. So the modeller and the rigger are
     sharing one document whether or not the UI admits it.

  2. THE SPINE. The handful of interactions that matter most, described
     as things a person does, in order, from opening a file to finishing
     a shot. Concrete: "right-drag orbits, scrolling zooms, clicking a
     bone selects it, R keys rotation at the current frame". This
     section is the point of the document.

  3. THE LAYOUT, as ASCII wireframes in a fenced block. One diagram per
     mode or screen, 16:9, with the panels labelled, and a note on what
     is fixed and what can be hidden. Plain text diagrams, please - this
     doc has to be readable in a terminal.

  4. PANEL INVENTORY AND PRIORITY. Every capability from the list above,
     mapped to where it lives in your design, marked P0 / P1 / P2. The
     test: nothing may be silently dropped. If you want to cut something,
     cut it explicitly, in its own section, with the reason.

  5. WHAT YOU WOULD DROP AND WHY. The s&box chrome, the panels that exist
     because a dock system made them easy, the dialogs that could be
     inline, the modal flows that should be direct manipulation. Be
     specific and be willing to be wrong out loud.

  6. MODES OR NO MODES, and how you keep a user from getting lost between
     modelling, rigging, animating and painting. Argue the trade-off.

  7. THE PERFORMANCE BUDGET. What the UI may spend per frame, what is
     cached, what rebuilds when a document changes, what happens while a
     12.8 s cold rebuild is running, and what a 419-body / 87k-triangle
     document does to your panel that lists bodies. A list of 419 rows is
     a real UI problem, not an afterthought.

  8. A BUILD ORDER. What an implementer does first, second, third, in
     dependency order, with the smallest first step that puts something
     usable on screen. Steps 5 through 8 of TASKS.md are the existing
     order; extend or argue with it.

  9. OPEN QUESTIONS FOR JOSHUA. Only the things you cannot decide from
     the code - how he actually works. Does he key with the mouse or the
     keyboard, does he start from a dope sheet or a curve graph, does he
     animate to reference video, does he want one window or two monitors,
     does he want to sculpt at all. Ask them as short questions he can
     answer in a line each.

RULES
  - Specs only, in specs/. No source edits anywhere, and no commits.
  - When you cite existing code, cite real types and methods with
    file:line, as you did in imgui-port.md.
  - When you assert a number, either cite the measured one from HANDOFF.md
    or mark it as an estimate and say it is one.
  - Flag anything you could not determine instead of filling the gap with
    something plausible. That is the failure mode this lane exists to
    avoid.
  - Do not write anything in Joshua's voice, and do not send any message
    for him.
  - Length: as long as it needs. One well-argued page on the spine is
    worth more than ten on widgets.

  USE THE RIGHT DOCUMENT FOR THE RIGHT QUESTION. These are measured, and
  they are the documents that decide the panel and list problems you are
  designing for:

    cookin/Assets/models/players/camhead.effigy
        617 bodies, 63,236 triangles, 15 material batches.
        9.9 s to load cold, 0.17 s warm.
        THIS IS THE DOCUMENT JOSHUA ACTUALLY CARES ABOUT, and the one to
        design against. It is his own character, and it is the row-count
        problem at its worst. Read the body names and notice what they
        are: "Spinous 4 arch", "Lumbar 5 facet R", "Ankle cheek R",
        "Heel hub L nut", "Hose elbow a hip top L", "Rotor bolt L 1",
        "Tilt motor", "Ab piston rod R". Anatomical regions, not loose
        parts - 617 rows that are all pieces of one body. Whatever your
        UI does with a list of bodies has to hold up against that.
        One more detail worth knowing: those 617 bodies collapse to only
        15 materials, so a list of materials is cheap and a list of BODIES
        is the expensive surface. And the colour you see is produced by a
        ten-line name matcher, MaterialPalette.cs, that was written for
        pod-engine names - chrome, brass, paint, soot. Eleven of
        camhead's fifteen materials match none of its rules and fall
        through to the same grey, lens_black included. That is why a
        screenshot of him is monochrome. It is also the gap a materials
        panel is supposed to close, and the palette is not worth porting.

    podracer/tools/effigy/engine.effigy
        419 bodies, 87,252 triangles, 12.8 s cold. The other stress case:
        a rocket engine with no rig, mostly kitbash detail. The opposite
        shape of document. Useful for "does this survive four hundred
        rows", useless for anything about a character.

  TWO THINGS TO KNOW BEFORE YOU ITERATE, both of them annoyances you
  will otherwise blame on your own changes:

    The geometry cache is keyed on the app's own kernel assembly, so
    REBUILDING THE APP INVALIDATES IT. You pay one 9.9 s first load after
    every build, then 0.17 s per load until you build again. The 9.9 s is
    not the UI being slow; it is a deliberate design trade recorded in
    HANDOFF.md section 10, and it is worth knowing before you conclude
    that something you wrote costs ten seconds.

    There are no .riganim clips for camhead, so testing clip playback
    means borrowing one. There are 135 clips on this machine: 74 under
